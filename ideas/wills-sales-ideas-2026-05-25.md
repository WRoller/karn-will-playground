## Common Threads — What Does This Say About What I Care About

A few patterns jump out when you look at this list honestly:

**You're drawn to the physical and experiential world, not pure digital.** Hospitality, outdoor gear, ocean experiences, fitness, a good meal, adventure kits — the bulk of your energy goes toward things people *feel* and *do* in the real world. Software shows up mainly as a means to make those things better, not as the end itself.

**You want to help people, but you're spread across very different "helps."** Mental health support, defense/warnings, conflict resolution, meditation — these sit next to sports platforms and board games. That's not incoherent, but it does suggest you haven't yet decided whether you want to *protect* people, *entertain* them, or *grow* them. It might be all three, but the business that tries to do all three at once usually does none of them well.

**Teaching and knowledge keep surfacing.** It shows up explicitly twice and implicitly in several others (business simulation, teachability as a criterion). There's a pull toward being the person who *explains* things or helps others get better — not just building a product and walking away.

**You're more interested in breadth of impact than depth in one niche.** The list is long. It covers food, fitness, geo-politics, IoT, healthcare, games, oceans, and space. That ambition is real, but the "Depth vs. Breadth" criterion you wrote down suggests you already sense the tension. Breadth is where your curiosity lives; depth is where the money and mastery usually are.

**The "value" question keeps nagging you — and that's a good sign.** You annotated vertical SW and teaching with "→ Value?" That self-interrogation matters. A lot of these ideas are things you'd *enjoy* working on, but you're clearly asking yourself which ones other people would actually pay for. Keep asking that question harder.

**Honestly, the list reads more like "things I find interesting" than "problems I'm uniquely positioned to solve."** That's fine for a brainstorm — it's supposed to be wide. But the next step is probably subtracting, not adding. Which of these do you have an unfair advantage in? Where do your skills, network, and obsessions overlap with a real gap in the market?
