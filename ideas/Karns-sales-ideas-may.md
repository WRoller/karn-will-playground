## What We Should NOT Build

### Businesses That Thin Human Connection
- Horizontal, mass-market software (generic CRMs, project management tools)
- Attention and engagement platforms (social media, ad-supported models, passive scrolling)
- Commodity / pure-arbitrage plays (race-to-the-bottom on price)
- Impersonal, scaled consumer products without community
- Purely transactional B2C without a community layer
- Healthcare as a core business (regulatory complexity + lack of domain fit — unless it's vertical software)
- Defense and weapons (macro interest, not personal conviction or expertise)
- Fast fashion / disposable goods

### The Core Principle to Avoid
> Anything that scales by making human connection thinner.

## Updated Themes — What's New in This Page

**Curation as a product.** The movies/music/3 cultures idea is distinct from making content — it's about being the taste layer. The value is the *selection* and *context*, not the production. Think Criterion Collection, not Netflix. This is a new and viable business model angle.

**Mindfulness/presence as a product.** "PRESENT" and "SINGLE-TASKING" are notable — there's a product or brand idea here around intentional living, focus, and depth in a distracted world. Could connect to the men's club / mental health thread from Will's page.

**Values and frameworks as the product itself.** "VALUES / FRAMES OF THINKING → value driven → why we do what we do" suggests the idea that your philosophy and worldview *is* what you sell — not just what you embed in a product. This is a media, publishing, or community play.

**Non-partisan politics / forcing confrontation with difference.** A platform or experience that novelty-forces people across cultural and political lines. Think: a dinner series, a media product, or a simulation. Maps to the "3 cultures" thread and the "different by design" note.

**Logistics research thread is deepening.** Flexport, Amazon, DoorDash/Uber, and the City of New York logistics question are all clustered together. This suggests growing conviction around the logistics/operations thesis from Will's page — not just as a sector to admire, but potentially to build in.

**The companies list is becoming more specific and refined.** Earlier pages had broader category names; this page names specific companies with distinct business models — Whoop (hardware + membership), Resy (niche vertical SaaS), Talenti (premium CPG with identity), Viator (marketplace for experiences). This is a more mature reference set.

## Revised Sweet Spot (Synthesizing All Three Pages)

The through-line across all sessions points to a tighter thesis:

> **Build niche, community-rooted products with a strong point of view — where the brand IS the worldview, and the product is the reason to gather.**

This shows up as:
- Curation businesses (taste + context across cultures)
- Vertical software for industries we actually care about (food, fitness, logistics)
- Physical or experiential community anchors (food, travel, spaces)
- Mindfulness/presence products in a distracted world
- Values-first brands that sell a frame of thinking, not just a thing
